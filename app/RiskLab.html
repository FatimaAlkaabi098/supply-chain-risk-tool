RiskLab
A practice workspace for CSF 4003 Security and Risk Management. You identify the assets and you decide every rating — the tool only does the arithmetic, and tells you when something does not look finished.
Everything runs in your browser and your work is saved on this device only. Use Print to hand in, or Export CSV to open in Excel.
Scales, formulas and thresholds follow the CSF 4003 slides (Chapters 1–3).

Glossary

Definitions as used in CSF 4003.

Information asset
Anything the organisation values: people, procedures, data and information, software, hardware, or networking elements.
Threat
A potential risk to an information asset. A threat is the possibility; an attack is it actually happening.
Attack
An ongoing act against an asset that could result in a loss.
Threat agent
The actor or attacker who uses an exploit to take advantage of a vulnerability.
Vulnerability
A specific opportunity that a threat agent can exploit to attack an information asset — a weakness where controls are absent or no longer effective.
Exploit
The technique or tool used to take advantage of a vulnerability.
Control
A protection mechanism already in place that prevents or impedes exploitation.
Risk
The chance of loss to an information asset, formed by a threat acting on a vulnerability.
Risk management
Discovering and assessing the risks to an organisation's operations and determining how they can be controlled or mitigated.
Risk identification
Listing and prioritising information assets, threats and vulnerabilities.
CIA triangle
Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability — the three core characteristics that give information value.
Confidentiality
Limiting access to information to authorised individuals only.
Integrity
Only authorised individuals can change or delete the data.
Availability
Information is there when an authorised person asks for it.
Weighted factor analysis
Ranking assets by scoring each against weighted criteria, then sorting by the total weighted score.
TVA worksheet
Threats–Vulnerabilities–Assets. The table pairing each asset with the threats it faces and the vulnerability each threat would use.
Likelihood
The probability that a threat will successfully exploit a vulnerability, and how often that might occur.
Impact / Severity
How much harm results if the exploit succeeds.
Risk rating
Likelihood × Impact, giving a score from 1 to 25.
Qualitative analysis
Rating risk with descriptive levels rather than money. Practical when data or budget is limited.
Quantitative analysis
Rating risk with hard numbers and money, so it can be compared against the cost of a control.
SLE
Single Loss Expectancy — what one occurrence costs.
ARO
Annualised Rate of Occurrence — how many times a year it is expected.
ALE
Annualised Loss Expectancy = SLE × ARO — the expected cost per year.
Residual risk
The risk that remains after a control has been applied. It is rarely zero.
